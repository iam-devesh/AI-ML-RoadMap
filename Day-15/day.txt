### Essence of Linear Algebra (3Blue1Brown) – Summary: Chapters 1–4

The series is designed to build geometric intuition for linear algebra concepts, translating numbers and equations into visual movements in space.

**Chapter 1: Vectors, what even are they?**
- **Three Perspectives**: 
  - Physics: Arrows in space with a length (magnitude) and direction.
  - Computer Science: Ordered lists of numbers.
  - Mathematics: Objects that can be added together and scaled by numbers.
- **Geometric Intuition**: Vectors are arrows rooted at the origin of a coordinate system. Vector addition is moving head-to-tail, and scalar multiplication is stretching or squishing the arrow.

**Chapter 2: Linear combinations, span, and basis vectors**
- **Basis Vectors**: The fundamental vectors of a coordinate system, typically \(\hat{i}\) (1,0) pointing right, and \(\hat{j}\) (0,1) pointing up. Every other vector is just a scaled combination of these two.
- **Linear Combination**: Scaling two or more vectors and adding them together (\(a\vec{v} + b\vec{w}\)).
- **Span**: The set of all possible points you can reach by taking linear combinations of your vectors. For most 2D pairs, the span is the entire 2D plane.
- **Linear Independence**: Vectors are linearly independent if each adds a new dimension to the span. If one can be written as a combination of others, they are linearly dependent.

**Chapter 3: Linear transformations and matrices**
- **Linear Transformations**: Functions that take an input vector and move it to an output vector. Geometrically, it's a transformation of the entire space.
- **Rules of Linearity**: Grid lines must remain parallel and evenly spaced, and the origin must stay fixed in place.
- **Matrices**: A numerical way to describe a linear transformation. By just recording where the basis vectors (\(\hat{i}\) and \(\hat{j}\)) land, you can determine where *any* vector in space will land. The columns of a matrix tell you the landing coordinates of the basis vectors.

**Chapter 4: Matrix multiplication as composition**
- **Composition**: Applying one linear transformation, followed by another. The net result is equivalent to a single, combined linear transformation.
- **Matrix Multiplication**: The mathematical process of computing the single matrix that represents this composition.
- **Order Matters**: By convention, matrix multiplication is read from right to left (like function application \(f(g(x))\)), meaning the rightmost matrix is the transformation applied first.
